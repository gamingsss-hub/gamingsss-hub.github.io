What Is a Formula Hub?

A formula hub is an educational reference library that brings important mathematical and scientific equations together in one place. Students can use these formulas while learning concepts, solving numerical problems and preparing for examinations.

Toolora Formula Hub covers five subjects: Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Statistics and Finance. Each subject includes useful equations and explanations to help learners understand how formulas are used.

Important Mathematics Formulas

Mathematics formulas are used in algebra, geometry, coordinate geometry, percentages and financial calculations. Understanding the variables in an equation makes numerical problems easier.

Quadratic Formula

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a

Finds the roots of ax² + bx + c = 0, where a is non-zero.

Area of a Circle

A = πr²

A is area and r is the radius of the circle.

Circle Circumference

C = 2πr

Calculates the distance around a circle.

Triangle Area

A = ½bh

b is the base and h is the perpendicular height.

Pythagorean Theorem

a² + b² = c²

Relates the sides of a right-angled triangle.

Distance Formula

d = √((x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²)

Calculates the distance between two coordinate points.

Midpoint Formula

M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)

Finds the midpoint between two points.

Slope of a Line

m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁)

Calculates slope when x₂ is not equal to x₁.

Simple Interest

SI = (P × R × T) / 100

P is principal, R is annual rate and T is time in years.

Volume of a Cube

V = a³

a is the side length of the cube.

Percentage

(Part / Whole) × 100

Converts a part of a total into a percentage.

Important Physics Formulas

Physics equations describe motion, forces, energy, electricity, waves and properties of matter. Always use consistent units when substituting values.

Average Speed

Speed = Distance / Time

Calculates distance travelled per unit time.

Acceleration

a = (v − u) / t

v is final velocity, u is initial velocity and t is time.

Newton's Second Law

F = ma

Net force equals mass multiplied by acceleration.

Momentum

p = mv

Momentum is the product of mass and velocity.

Work Done

W = Fd cos θ

Work done by a constant force over displacement d.

Power

P = W / t

Power is the rate of doing work.

Ohm's Law

V = IR

Relates voltage, current and resistance.

Weight

F = mg

Calculates gravitational force on an object.

Gravitational Potential Energy

PE = mgh

m is mass, g is gravitational acceleration and h is height.

Kinetic Energy

KE = ½mv²

Energy associated with an object's motion.

Wave Speed

v = fλ

f is frequency and λ is wavelength.

Frequency

f = 1 / T

Frequency is the reciprocal of time period.

Universal Gravitation

F = Gm₁m₂ / r²

Describes gravitational attraction between two masses.

Density

ρ = m / V

Density is mass per unit volume.

Important Chemistry Formulas

Chemistry formulas help describe gases, solutions, concentration, acids and bases, and chemical properties of substances.

Ideal Gas Law

PV = nRT

Relates pressure, volume, moles and absolute temperature.

Molarity

M = n / V

n is moles of solute and V is solution volume in litres.

Mass Density

ρ = m / V

Mass divided by volume.

Charles's Law

V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂

Applies to a fixed amount of gas at constant pressure.

pH Formula

pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]

Expresses hydrogen ion concentration on the pH scale.

Beer-Lambert Law

A = εlc

Relates absorbance to path length and concentration.

Important Statistics Formulas

Statistics formulas are useful for analysing numerical data, calculating averages, measuring variation and understanding probability.

Arithmetic Mean

Mean = Σx / n

Sum of observations divided by their count.

Basic Probability

P(A) = Favourable / Total

For equally likely outcomes.

Z-Score

z = (x − μ) / σ

Measures distance from the mean in standard deviations.

Population Variance

σ² = Σ(x − μ)² / N

Measures average squared deviation from the population mean.

Important Finance Formulas

Finance formulas are commonly used in savings, interest, business calculations, discounts and profit analysis.

Compound Interest

A = P(1 + r/n)ⁿᵗ

Calculates future value with periodic compounding.

Profit

Profit = SP − CP

Selling price minus cost price.

Profit Percentage

(Profit / CP) × 100

Expresses profit as a percentage of cost price.

Discount

Discount = MP × Rate / 100

Calculates the reduction from marked price.
